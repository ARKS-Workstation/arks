# Positioning Strategy

## 1. The wedge

The AI-in-HR conversation on LinkedIn is saturated with consultants, vendors, and
analysts. It is almost entirely written by and for people whose workforce sits at a desk
with a laptop.

Your workforce sits behind a wheel.

That is the whole position. You are not competing in "AI thought leadership." You are
claiming an uncontested adjacent category:

> **AI adoption for frontline, multilingual, non-desk workforces — proven in live
> operations, not in a pilot deck.**

Almost nobody publishing in this space has actually deployed an AI assistant to people
who do not have corporate email, in 45°C heat, across language barriers, where a bad
answer has physical consequences. You have. That is the moat.

## 2. Positioning statement

Use this as the north star for every post. If a draft doesn't ladder to it, cut it.

> I run people and fleet operations for an electric transport company in Dubai. My
> workforce doesn't sit at desks — they sit behind wheels. I build and deploy AI
> assistants into that reality, and I publish what it actually costs to get it right.

## 3. Audience map

You selected all three audiences in rotation. That is a broader net than I'd normally
recommend, so the calendar is engineered to keep them from diluting each other: every
post is written for **one** primary audience, with a **secondary hook** that keeps the
other two reading. The three never share a post equally.

| # | Audience | What they want from you | What they do next |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | HR & People leaders adopting AI | Proof AI works outside the knowledge-worker bubble; frameworks they can copy | Follow, save, DM for a call |
| B | GCC fleet, mobility & sustainability operators | Unit economics, real operating data, regional reality | Connect, partnership inbound |
| C | Founders & ops leaders buying/building AI | Build notes, failure modes, what production actually demands | Follow, advisory/consulting inbound |

**Rotation weighting across 30 posts:** A = 12 · B = 9 · C = 6 · Cross-cutting = 3.

Audience A carries the majority because it is the largest engagement pool on LinkedIn
and matches your stated goal. B and C convert at higher intent but reach fewer people —
they are your commercial layer, not your growth layer.

## 4. Content pillars

### Pillar 1 — Frontline AI *(Audience A · 12 posts)*
AI adoption for people without laptops. Onboarding, training, compliance, retention,
language, trust. Your flagship pillar.

### Pillar 2 — The Electric Fleet *(Audience B · 9 posts)*
EV fleet unit economics, charging as a rostering problem, driver behaviour vs. vehicle
spec, heat, TCO, GCC policy and sustainability reporting.

### Pillar 3 — Build Notes: Max & Yulisa *(Audience C · 6 posts)*
What it takes to ship an AI assistant into live operations and keep it alive. Failure
modes, escalation design, cost of ownership, measurement against a human baseline.

### Pillar 4 — The Operator's Desk *(Cross-cutting · 3 posts)*
Leadership POV, hiring philosophy, contrarian takes, and the periodic reflection post.
Low volume by design — this pillar earns trust, it does not build it.

## 5. Rotation logic

The calendar never runs the same pillar twice in a row, and never runs the same *format*
twice in a row. This does two things:

1. **Algorithmic**: LinkedIn's feed rewards format variety on a single author profile.
   Three carousels in a row trains the algorithm to treat you as a single-format account.
2. **Human**: a follower who came for EV economics stays because the HR post two days
   later was also good. That is how three audiences become one audience.

## 6. What you are optimising for

Not follower count. In order of priority:

1. **Profile views from target-title accounts** (HR Director, Head of People, Fleet
   Manager, COO, Founder). This is the real signal.
2. **Saves and shares** — the only metrics LinkedIn weights that indicate durable value.
3. **Qualified DMs and connection requests** with a stated reason.
4. **Comment depth** — a 40-word comment from a Head of People is worth 200 likes.
5. Follower growth, last.

## 7. Guardrails

- Never publish a metric you cannot defend if a journalist calls.
- Never name a client, contract value, or individual driver without written consent.
- Never post a failure story that identifies the person who made the mistake.
- If a post could be read as a vendor pitch for Max or Yulisa, add a line of cost or
  failure. Credibility is bought with disclosed cost.
