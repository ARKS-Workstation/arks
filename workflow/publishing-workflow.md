# Recurring Workflow

Everything below is a repeatable loop. Total time commitment: **~2.5 hours per week**,
concentrated into one batching session plus short daily engagement windows.

---

## A. Weekly batch session — Sunday, 90 minutes

Do this once. It removes the daily decision of "what do I post today", which is what
kills every content cadence.

- [ ] Open `calendar/60-day-calendar.md`, read the next 3–4 scheduled posts
- [ ] Pull the real numbers you'll need this week from your own systems — do this first,
      before writing. Writing around a number you haven't checked produces a post you
      can't publish.
- [ ] Draft all posts for the week into `drafts/`
- [ ] Build any carousel or graphic assets
- [ ] Run the pre-publish checklist (Section B) on each
- [ ] Schedule or queue them

**Rule:** never write a post the morning it publishes. Same-day writing is where the
placeholder-shaped mistakes and the unclear-what-I-can-disclose mistakes happen.

---

## B. Pre-publish checklist — per post, 5 minutes

### Substance
- [ ] Zero `[[BRACKETS]]` remain. Run: `grep -o '\[\[[^]]*\]\]' <file>`
- [ ] Every number is one you can defend if challenged publicly
- [ ] No client, contract value, or individual is identifiable
- [ ] Any failure story describes a process gap, not a named person's mistake
- [ ] If this post could read as a pitch for Max or Yulisa, it contains a disclosed cost
      or failure

### Craft
- [ ] First 210 characters work standalone — paste them alone and reread
- [ ] Opening line is not a greeting, an announcement, or "In today's world"
- [ ] One idea only
- [ ] Single-line paragraphs throughout, generous white space
- [ ] Closes with a question you actually want answered
- [ ] Read it aloud once. Anything you'd never say out loud gets cut

### Mechanics
- [ ] 3–5 hashtags, `#FrontlineAI` included
- [ ] No external link in the body — link goes in first comment if needed
- [ ] Media attached and correct: carousel is PDF at 1080×1350, screenshots redacted
- [ ] Alt text written for every image (accessibility, and it is indexed)
- [ ] Spellcheck run — a typo in the first line is disproportionately costly
- [ ] Anyone tagged has been asked first

## C. Publish day

| Time | Action |
|---|---|
| **07:30 GST** | Publish. Tue–Thu strongest; avoid Friday entirely; Sunday is viable in the Gulf but weaker for European reach. |
| **07:30–08:30** | **The golden hour.** This is the only hour that determines the post's reach. |
| **+0 min** | Post the first comment yourself — an extra data point, a caveat, or the link. Never leave your own comment section empty. |
| **+5 min** | Notify 5–10 peers directly. Ask for disagreement, not support: *"I made a claim about X here — think I'm wrong?"* |
| **+0 to 60 min** | Reply to **every** comment, each reply ending in a question. Reply length should roughly match comment length. |
| **+60 min** | Stop. Do not edit the post — editing resets distribution. |
| **+4 hrs** | Second engagement sweep: new comments, then 20 minutes commenting substantively on *other* people's posts. |
| **+24 hrs** | Final reply sweep. Log metrics (Section E). |

### The golden hour is non-negotiable
LinkedIn tests a post with a small audience in the first 60–90 minutes and expands
distribution based on the engagement rate it observes. A post that gets ten comments in
hour one and forty in day two will reach a fraction of the audience of the same post with
those numbers reversed. If you cannot be present for the hour after publishing, publish
on a different day.

## D. Ongoing engagement — 20 minutes daily

Content alone will not grow the account. Roughly 40% of your growth will come from
comments left on other people's posts, because that is where a new audience discovers you.

- [ ] Comment on **5 posts per day** from your target audiences
- [ ] Rule: every comment must add information the original post did not contain — a
      counter-example, a number, a distinction. Never "Great insight!"
- [ ] Aim for 30–60 words. Long enough to be substantive, short enough to be read.
- [ ] Maintain a target list of ~30 accounts: HR leaders, GCC fleet operators, mobility
      journalists, AI-ops founders. Engage with the same people repeatedly — recognition
      compounds.
- [ ] Accept relevant connection requests with a one-line reply, not silently.
- [ ] Answer every DM within 24 hours, even if the answer is "not right now."

**Your headline is doing marketing on every one of these comments.** That is why Section 1
of the profile doc matters more than it looks.

## E. Analytics — what to track and what to ignore

Log weekly in a simple sheet. Ten minutes.

### Track these

| Metric | Where | Target (by day 60) | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Profile views from target titles** | Analytics → Profile viewers → job titles | Steady week-on-week rise | The truest signal. Ten Heads of People beats a thousand anonymous views. |
| **Saves per post** | Post analytics | 2%+ of impressions | The strongest indicator of durable value. Saves predict what to write more of. |
| **Reposts / shares** | Post analytics | 1%+ of impressions | The only metric that reaches genuinely new audiences. |
| **Comment depth** | Manual | 3+ comments over 40 words per post | A long comment from a senior operator outweighs 200 reactions. |
| **Qualified DMs** | Manual | 4+ per month | The commercial outcome. This is why you're doing it. |
| **Follower quality** | Analytics → Followers → job title breakdown | Rising share of HR/fleet/founder titles | Growth in the wrong titles is worse than no growth. |
| **Post-to-profile rate** | Impressions vs. profile views | 1%+ | Measures whether your posts make people curious about *you*. |

### Ignore these

- **Impressions.** Volatile, mostly a function of when you posted, and uncorrelated with
  outcomes.
- **Reactions.** Free to give and therefore near-worthless as signal.
- **Total follower count.** A vanity number that will tempt you into writing broader,
  blander posts.
- **Any single post's performance.** Judge in blocks of ten. Individual post variance on
  LinkedIn is enormous and mostly noise.

### The review rhythm

| When | Review |
|---|---|
| **Weekly** | Which post got the most saves? Write more in that shape. |
| **Every 10 posts** | Pillar performance. Which of P1–P4 is actually landing? Reweight the remaining calendar toward it. |
| **Day 30** | Full audit. Headline switch decision (Option 1 → Option 5). Refresh Featured section. Request recommendations. |
| **Day 60** | Post 30 is the public recap. Privately: rebuild the next calendar from the top 5 posts by saves, not by reach. |

## F. Failure modes to watch for

| Symptom | Likely cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Reach collapses on one post | External link in body, or the post was edited within the first hour | Never link in body; never edit early |
| High impressions, no profile views | Post is interesting; you are not | Add more first-person operational specifics |
| Comments are all "Great post!" | CTA is too open or too flattering | Ask a contestable question, or ask for disagreement |
| Followers growing, wrong titles | Pillar 4 posts over-indexing | Cut general leadership content, return to operational specifics |
| Cadence slipping | Writing same-day instead of batching | Return to Section A. This is always the cause. |
| Post feels like a pitch | Missing disclosed cost or failure | Add the failure paragraph. It is mandatory for a reason. |

---

## G. The one-line version

Batch on Sunday. Publish 07:30. Own the golden hour. Comment on five posts a day.
Track saves, not reactions. Never publish a number you can't defend.
