## 1. Headline — 5 options

LinkedIn indexes the headline for search and shows it everywhere you appear: feed,
comments, search results, connection requests. It is the single highest-leverage field
on the profile. Limit: **220 characters.**

Two jobs, in this order: (1) contain the keywords your target audience searches,
(2) make a specific claim nobody else on LinkedIn can make.

### Option 1 — Role + proof + category *(recommended)*

> Head of People & Operations at Ecosine Transports | Deploying AI assistants to a
> frontline EV fleet workforce in Dubai | HR × AI × Sustainable Mobility

**148 characters.**
**Rationale:** Leads with a verifiable role (credibility), states the specific thing you
do that others don't (differentiation), and closes with three searchable keyword clusters
(discovery). The phrase "frontline EV fleet workforce" is doing the heaviest lifting —
it signals to Audiences A, B and C simultaneously without reading as a list. This is the
one I'd run.

### Option 2 — Outcome-led

> I build AI systems that train and support drivers, not desk workers | People & Fleet
> Operations, Ecosine Transports | EV fleets, Dubai

**134 characters.**
**Rationale:** Opening in first person is unusual in a headline and stops the scroll.
"not desk workers" is the whole positioning in three words. Weaker on search keywords
than Option 1 — "HR" never appears, which costs you recruiter and People-leader search
traffic.

### Option 3 — Category claim

> Frontline AI, not office AI | HR & EV Fleet Operations Leader, Dubai | Built Max and
> Yulisa — AI assistants for driver training and operational support

**156 characters.**
**Rationale:** Strongest if you intend to commercialise Max and Yulisa, because it makes
them named assets rather than internal tools. Naming products in a headline builds
recall — people will start referring to "the Max guy." Risk: it reads slightly
promotional, which sits a little outside the executive register you selected.

### Option 4 — Maximum search coverage

> HR & People Operations | AI Implementation | EV Fleet Management | Sustainable
> Transport | Dubai, UAE

**103 characters.**
**Rationale:** Pure keyword density. This is what you use if inbound recruiter and
procurement search is the priority over thought leadership. It ranks well and says
nothing memorable. Included for completeness — I would not run it given your stated goal.

### Option 5 — Provocative

> Most AI pilots serve the 10% of the workforce with a laptop. I work on the other 90%. |
> People & Fleet Ops, Ecosine Transports | Dubai

**138 characters.**
**Rationale:** Highest stopping power of the five. Every time you comment on someone
else's post, this argument appears under your name — which means your headline is doing
content marketing for you across the platform. Risk: a strong claim you must keep earning.
Use it only if you are committed to the full 60-day cadence.

**Verdict:** Run **Option 1** for the first 30 days while you build a body of work. Switch
to **Option 5** once you have 10+ posts of evidence behind it.

## 2. About section — full rewrite

Limit 2,600 characters. Mobile truncates at roughly **250 characters** behind a "…see
more" — so the first three lines must work as a standalone advertisement for the rest.

Structure used below: **Claim → Context → Proof → Systems → Invitation.**

```
Most organisations are piloting AI for the ten percent of their workforce that sits
at a desk.

I work on the other ninety percent.

I lead people and fleet operations at Ecosine Transports in Dubai, where our workforce
is [[HEADCOUNT, e.g. 120]] professional drivers operating a fleet of 140
electric vehicles. They do not have corporate email. They work across
[[NUMBER]] languages. They operate in 45°C heat on schedules where a wrong answer is
not an inconvenience — it is a safety event.

That environment does not tolerate AI theatre. It is the reason the systems we build
have to be genuinely good.

Over the past [[TIMEFRAME, e.g. 18 months]] I have designed and deployed two AI
assistants into live operations:

→ Max — driver training and competency development. Onboarding time moved from
  [[BEFORE]] to [[AFTER]], and [[METRIC, e.g. first-90-day retention]] improved by
  [[FIGURE]].

→ Yulisa — real-time operational support for drivers and dispatch. Now handles
  [[VOLUME]] queries per [[PERIOD]], with a deliberate escalation path to a human for
  anything safety-related.

Alongside that, I run the operating side of an electric fleet: charging strategy,
route and roster optimisation, driver behaviour programmes, and the sustainability
reporting that comes with it. Over [[TIMEFRAME]] we have moved
[[CORE METRIC — e.g. cost per kilometre / energy per km / utilisation]] by [[FIGURE]].

WHAT I WRITE ABOUT HERE

▪ Deploying AI to frontline, non-desk, multilingual workforces — including what fails
▪ The real unit economics of running an EV fleet in the Gulf
▪ Building AI assistants that survive contact with live operations
▪ Building HR systems that operations teams will actually use

I publish every other day. Everything I share comes from systems I have personally
run, with the costs included — not from a vendor deck.

If you are deploying AI into a frontline workforce, electrifying a fleet in the
region, or building operational AI and want to compare notes on what breaks — my DMs
are open.

[[EMAIL]] · Dubai, UAE
```

**Rationale, section by section:**

- **The first two lines are the entire strategy.** They make a specific, arguable claim
  and immediately position you on the underserved side of it. This is what survives
  mobile truncation, so it is the only part most readers will see. Everything else is for
  the minority who tap "see more" — and they are the ones who matter.
- **The constraint paragraph** (no corporate email, multiple languages, 45°C, safety
  consequences) is your credibility engine. It is specific, unusual, and impossible to
  fake. Consultants cannot write this paragraph.
- **The arrow-bulleted systems** give the reader two named, concrete assets. Named things
  are memorable; "AI initiatives" are not.
- **"including what fails"** and **"with the costs included"** are deliberate. In an
  executive register, disclosed cost is the cheapest credibility you can buy.
- **"I publish every other day"** sets an expectation that converts profile visitors into
  followers. Do not include this line unless you will actually hold the cadence.
- **The CTA is qualified, not open.** "DMs are open" alone attracts noise. Naming three
  specific situations filters for the people you want.

## 3. Experience section

The Experience block is read by two audiences with opposite needs: humans skimming for
credibility, and LinkedIn's search index looking for keywords. Write for both by leading
each bullet with a verb and ending it with a number.

### Ecosine Transports — [[YOUR TITLE]] · [[DATES]] · Dubai, UAE

**One-line role summary (put this first, before the bullets):**

> People and operations lead for an electric transport fleet — accountable for
> [[HEADCOUNT]] drivers, 140 vehicles, and the AI systems that support both.

**Bullets:**

```
▪ Designed and deployed Max, an AI-driven driver training and competency system,
  reducing onboarding time from [[BEFORE]] to [[AFTER]] and improving
  [[RETENTION/COMPETENCY METRIC]] by [[FIGURE]].

▪ Built and operationalised Yulisa, an AI operational support assistant handling
  [[VOLUME]] driver and dispatch queries per [[PERIOD]], with defined human escalation
  for all safety-critical cases.

▪ Rebuilt the end-to-end hiring and onboarding pipeline for a non-desk, multilingual
  workforce, cutting time-to-productivity by [[FIGURE]] across [[NUMBER]] languages.

▪ Own charging strategy and roster optimisation for a 140-vehicle EV fleet,
  improving [[UTILISATION / ENERGY PER KM / COST PER KM]] by [[FIGURE]].

▪ Introduced a driver-behaviour programme linking telematics data to coaching,
  delivering [[FIGURE]] improvement in [[EFFICIENCY / INCIDENT RATE]].

▪ Established the fleet's sustainability reporting baseline, producing
  [[REPORT TYPE, e.g. monthly CO₂e avoided per route]] for [[STAKEHOLDER]].

▪ Reduced [[COST CENTRE]] by [[FIGURE]] through [[LEVER]] while headcount grew
  [[FIGURE]].
```

**Rationale:** Seven bullets is the ceiling — past that, nobody reads. The order is
deliberate: AI first (your differentiator), HR second (your category), fleet third (your
proof), cost last (what an executive reader is actually scanning for). Each bullet is a
potential post topic, which is not a coincidence — your Experience section and your
content calendar should describe the same body of work.

## 4. The rest of the profile, ranked by leverage

| # | Element | Action | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Featured section** | Pin 3: your best long-form post, a Max/Yulisa case study, and one EV fleet data post. Refresh every 30 days. | The only part of the profile you fully control below the fold. Converts visitors into followers. |
| 2 | **Banner image** | 1584×396. Fleet photography — real vehicles, real depot — with a single overlaid line: *"AI for the workforce that doesn't sit at a desk."* | A stock-gradient banner signals a profile nobody maintains. Real operational photography signals the opposite. |
| 3 | **Profile photo** | Head and shoulders, neutral background, natural light, no vehicle in frame. Warm, direct, not corporate-stiff. | It appears at 40px in the feed. Anything busy becomes noise. |
| 4 | **Custom URL** | `linkedin.com/in/[[firstname-lastname]]` — remove the trailing digits. | Free credibility on every business card, email signature, and CV. |
| 5 | **Skills (top 3 pinned)** | `Artificial Intelligence (AI)` · `Human Resources (HR)` · `Fleet Management` | The top 3 pinned skills are weighted in LinkedIn search. Pin the ones you want to be found for, not the ones you're best at. |
| 6 | **Creator mode / topics** | Set hashtags: `#FrontlineAI` `#HRTech` `#EVFleet` `#PeopleOperations` `#SustainableTransport` | Tells the algorithm which feed to surface you in, and unlocks the Follow-first button. |
| 7 | **Recommendations** | Request 3: one operations peer, one direct report, one external partner. Give each a sentence to anchor on. | Three specific recommendations beat thirty generic endorsements. Ask in week 2, not week 8. |
| 8 | **Open to** | Set to *Providing services* → `HR Consulting`, `Business Consulting`, `Training`. | Adds a services page to your profile and makes you appear in LinkedIn's services search — a channel almost nobody in your space uses. |

## 5. Sequencing

Do not change everything at once. LinkedIn briefly suppresses reach on profiles with
simultaneous large edits, and you lose the ability to attribute what worked.

| When | Change |
|---|---|
| Day 0 (before Post 1) | Headline, About, custom URL, photo, banner |
| Day 7 | Experience bullets, skills pinning |
| Day 14 | Featured section (populate with your first 3 posts) |
| Day 21 | Recommendation requests, Open-to services |
| Day 30 | Review headline performance; consider Option 1 → Option 5 switch |
