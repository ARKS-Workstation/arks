Every EV fleet business case I have reviewed models the same four things: vehicle capex, energy, maintenance, and residual value.

Here are nine more that we learned about the expensive way.

→ Grid connection. Not the chargers — the supply behind them. Transformer, cabling, utility works, and a lead time measured in months rather than weeks.

→ Peak demand charges. Your model multiplies kWh by a rate. Your utility also bills you on the highest simultaneous draw you ever hit. Twenty vehicles charging at once sets a number you keep paying against.

→ Civil works. Trenching, ducting, bollards, drainage, re-marking bays, fire separation. The chargers are the cheap part. Putting them in the ground is not.

→ Charger maintenance and downtime. Chargers are assets and they fail. The real cost is not the repair — it is the bay you cannot roster against while it is out.

→ Thermal management. Conditioning the pack and pre-cooling the cabin draws energy that never appears in the kWh-per-km figure on the spec sheet. In a Gulf summer that is not a rounding error.

→ Charging losses. The energy your meter bills you for and the energy that reaches the battery are not the same number. You pay the difference on every charge, forever.

→ Parts lead time. EV supply chains are thinner. A fault that puts a diesel off the road for two days can put an EV off the road for weeks. That is a revenue line, not a maintenance line.

→ Retraining. High-voltage certification for workshop staff, and re-training drivers on regenerative braking and charging discipline. It recurs every time headcount turns over.

→ Warranty conditions. Battery warranties constrain how you may charge — fast-charge frequency, state-of-charge limits. That is a cost in operational flexibility, and it lands on your roster.

Three of these were larger than our maintenance saving. If your model doesn't have them, your model is optimistic.

## Two structural changes I made

**The nine are no longer a single middot-separated line.** Nine items strung across one
line is a wall of text on a phone and gives the reader nothing to hold. Each is now a
name plus one clause explaining why it gets missed — which is the actual value, and
which also gives your infographic its nine rows.

**Each line names a mechanism, not a category.** "Grid connection" alone is a heading
anyone could write. "Not the chargers — the supply behind them" is the sentence that
proves you have been through it.

## What you must confirm before publishing

These nine are the cost lines that genuinely get left out of EV fleet models. They are
domain fact, not invented. But the post says **we** learned them the expensive way, which
is a claim about Ecosine, not about the industry.

- [ ] Each of the nine actually bit you. Cut any that did not — eight real ones beat
      nine where one is borrowed. Change the title to match the count.
- [ ] **"Three of these were larger than our maintenance saving."** This is the sharpest
      line in the post and the only quantified claim in it. You need to be able to name
      those three if asked. If you cannot, soften to "Several of these were larger than
      we forecast" — still true, still strong, and defensible.
- [ ] The Gulf thermal line is your differentiator. Keep it near the middle where a
      skim-reader still lands on it.

## Candidates I left out, and why

| Line | Why it did not make the nine |
|---|---|
| Charge-management software subscriptions | Real, but small, and reads as procurement trivia next to grid connection. |
| Public charging and roaming fees | Only bites fleets that overrun their depot capacity. Not universal enough. |
| Depot space opportunity cost | Genuinely underrated, but hard to state in one clause without sounding theoretical. |
| Utility standing charges | Overlaps with peak demand and would dilute it. |
| Battery state-of-health at resale | Sits inside residual value, which is one of the four everyone already models. |

Any of these can swap in for a line that did not apply to you — the first two are the
strongest substitutes.
